About Redirect Import

    
Turns a sheet of old and new addresses into the exact file your platform imports, and catches what would break the import before you upload it.


    
How to use it

    
    
      
  1. Copy the two columns from Excel or Google Sheets, and press Ctrl + V anywhere on the page, or drop a CSV or Excel file on it.
    2. 
      
  2. Pick where you’re importing them.
    3. 
      
  3. Fix anything marked red in your sheet. The file beside it changes as you type.
    4. 
      
  4. Download the file, or copy it, and import it on the platform.
    5. 
    


    

      
What it fixes for you

      
    
        
  • Removes the domain from old addresses, and from new ones on your own site (addresses on other sites keep theirs).
    • 
        
  • Removes tracking tags (utm_ and similar) and the # part from old addresses.
    • 
        
  • Writes accented letters the way browsers send them (é becomes %C3%A9).
    • 
        
  • Leaves out rows that point to themselves, repeat another row, or are marked “disregard”, “skip”, “delete”, “404” and the like instead of a new address.
    • 
        
  • Splits columns pasted with spaces between them, as from an email or PDF.
    • 
      

    


    

      
What it asks you to fix

      
    
        
  • Addresses cut short with …, as reports show long ones (copying the text copies the shortened address).
    • 
        
  • New addresses on a preview or staging site (myshopify.com, webflow.io, wixsite.com, a staging subdomain, localhost and others), which would send visitors there once the site is live.
    • 
        
  • Spaces, Word’s long dashes and curly quotes, hidden tabs and line breaks, a stray %, and characters an address can’t hold (< > " { } | \ ^ `).
    • 
        
  • A missing old or new address.
    • 
        
  • The same old address sent to two different places (you pick which row to keep), and loops.
    • 
        
  • Chains, where one row moves A to B and another moves B to C, so visitors are redirected twice. They go in the file as you typed them, marked to check, with a button that sends A straight to C.
    • 
        
  • Each platform’s own rules, from its help pages: addresses Shopify keeps for itself (/cart, /shop, /apps and others), no ? in old addresses on Squarespace and Duda, no % on Duda, up to 140 characters on HubSpot, and files split into parts where an import has a cap (500 for Wix and HubSpot, 200 for Duda).
    • 
      

    


    

      
Where it can import

      
    
        
  • Shopify, Wix, Webflow, Framer (with its Redirect Sync plugin), HubSpot, BigCommerce and Duda: a CSV file.
    • 
        
  • Squarespace: lines to paste into URL mappings. Ghost: a redirects.yaml file.
    • 
        
  • WordPress: a CSV for the Redirection plugin, Rank Math (Pro) or Yoast SEO Premium.
    • 
        
  • Netlify (_redirects), Vercel (vercel.json, or a bulk redirects CSV), Apache (.htaccess) and Nginx.
    • 
        
  • Plain CSV, with any header row and separator, for anything else.
    • 
      

    


    
Common questions

    

      
Can I change the header row or separator?

      
Click the header line in the file and type. The separator follows what you type, and Format has the status column, full URLs and the rest.

      
Why is my file in parts?

      
Some platforms take a set number of redirects per import. Import each part in turn.

      
Is my sheet uploaded anywhere?

      
No. It’s read and checked in your browser, and kept there until you press Clear, so a reload doesn’t lose your edits.
