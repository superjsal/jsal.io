What’s changed

Everything that has changed on jsal.io, newest first. The home page keeps only the latest change for each part of the site.

1 November 2026

  • SiteOn phones, the browser’s bar takes the page’s colour, cream or slate, on every page rather than only the tools, and now follows the theme button too. Adding jsal.io to an Android home screen uses the logo’s tile as its icon.
  • SiteThe logo, refined: the icon’s j is lowercase at every size, wearing the gold gem as its dot, and the j in the name has round ends, the one soft letter in jsal.io. On the first page of a visit, the name opens as the tab icon: its short j stretches into the name’s while the gem hops over the name to land as its full stop.
  • SiteThe home page’s Platform guides show each platform’s logo beside its name, each line in Updates carries its app’s colour, and the Platform Playbook tile shows a small preview of its checks.
  • Sitejsal.io has a logo: a j on a slate tile, with the full stop cut as a gold gem. It’s the browser tab’s icon and the phone home-screen icon, and the name at the top of every page and in every link preview now ends in the same gem. On the first page of a visit, the name opens as the mark and unfolds into jsal.io.

31 October 2026

  • SiteOn a first visit, the logo menu on the CMS Picker, Clean Paste and Image Resizer opens as soon as the page shows, instead of waiting for the app’s own scripts to load; a click made while a page is still changing lands once its buttons work.
  • CMS PickerThe quiz box reserves its first question’s height, so the rows under it no longer jump when the page loads or between questions.
  • SiteEvery page passes the W3C markup checker: Clean Paste’s editor and the Playbook’s guide panel use elements their roles allow, and a hidden image has a source.
  • Image ResizerOn desktop, the mouse wheel over the settings column (and the empty space under it) scrolls the page again; it used to do nothing there.
  • CMS PickerOn a platform card, each “Not ideal if” reason now has its own dot, so two reasons no longer read as one run-on sentence.
  • SiteThe home page’s platform guides have the site’s standard corner instead of pill ends, so they read as an index of links rather than a row of buttons or choices. The list ends with a “Compare all 13” tile that fills out the last row, so the grid is even on every screen, set like the platform tiles with an arrow after the words.
  • CMS PickerEach platform page’s “What’s built in” list now includes selling memberships or paid posts, the one everyday row it was missing.
  • CMS PickerEvery answer, source and platform page was checked against each platform’s own documentation. Webflow’s own Localize add-on now counts as built-in languages, Drupal’s store cost names Drupal Commerce (free software), Squarespace’s note on hiding a page says a single post or product only hides with its whole blog or shop, Shopify and Webflow’s AI-crawler notes mention their newer controls, and several sources, menu paths and notes were brought up to date. The team Playbook got the same pass.
  • CMS PickerOn a result, the names under “Also worth a look” open “Had a platform in mind?” on that platform.
  • CMS PickerTwo or more languages is a tick beside whatever you need from day one, not one of the choices, so a restaurant can say bookings and two languages. When two builders are level, the one whose card leads with the design control you asked for comes first (Wix’s drag-and-drop editor, Squarespace’s designer templates), so a local business sees a clear best match far more often. The members choice now says paid subscribers or members, so a plain email newsletter doesn’t steer you to membership platforms.
  • CMS PickerThe quiz asks what you’ll need from day one (bookings, paid members, selling, a blog, another language) and whether you’re moving from another platform, in place of the questions on languages, members, moving later and adding features: eight questions at most. Moving from a platform shows what it lets you take with you, and a match whose own docs describe an import from it says so; the one you’re leaving isn’t offered as a match. Shared links from before still open on their result.
  • SiteThe small line under the top bar (the home page’s name line, a platform page’s breadcrumb, Changelog, Privacy) starts flush with the logo on every page, and the home page’s intro, buttons and platform guides sit flush with its heading, so nothing shifts sideways between pages.
  • SiteThe home page links each platform’s guide under the intro, in even columns level with the CMS Picker tile; on narrower screens the grid sits under both, and on a phone after the tile.
  • CMS PickerWanting hosting handled no longer counts as heavily against WordPress and Magento, whose own hosting lines name managed hosts that take updates and security on; half as many results are only a “closest match”.
  • CMS PickerA reason that comes from a sourced answer (languages, memberships, the cost band) links to the platform’s own page on it.
  • CMS PickerA “Compare these side by side” link at the top of the result as well as the bottom; on phones the table shows each platform’s fit label on its row, and a section’s count keeps clear of the Top button.
  • CMS PickerWhen two platforms score the same, the one whose card names what you’re making comes first (a startup, a local business, a large catalogue, a publication…), and the result says so; the rest stay joint. Each card now leads with the reasons the other two don’t share.
  • CMS PickerA platform page’s “Compare with others” opens the table with that platform in it and its column marked.
  • CMS PickerThe result says how sure it is (a close call, a clear lead or a clear winner, from the points between first and second), and “Had a platform in mind?” ranks any other platform for your answers with what counted for and against it. On phones, platform pickers use the phone’s own picker and the Compare chips sit in two even columns.
  • CMS PickerThe table’s two switches (All rows / Differences, Tags / Notes) look like switches rather than platform chips; section headings sit a step darker than a row your answers rule out; opening the cards or the table brings it into view.
  • SiteWarnings are terracotta instead of orange, so they never read as gold; light mode’s lines and wells are a touch cooler and firmer, its gold shows as gold on lines and selected edges, and its greens, blues and reds are livelier.
  • SiteThe shared styles are one file every page links, instead of a copy inside each page: pages are about a quarter smaller, and nothing looks different.
  • SiteMoving between pages is one quiet slide everywhere, with the top bar staying put; the Back button runs it the other way.
  • CMS PickerComparing from a quiz result lands on the result line with every section open; on a phone, rows your answers rule out fold to one line, as does the list of your answers above the matches. Shorter section headings in the table.
  • CMS PickerComparing from a quiz result carries it along: each column’s fit, a line on what put the best match ahead, rows your answers rule out dimmed, and an “Only differences” switch for the table.
  • SiteThe logo menu works again on a page you come back to with the Back button.
  • CMS PickerThe quiz comes first; the platform cards and the comparison table open when you ask for them.
  • CMS PickerPicking platforms to compare is clearer: a tick on each pick, a count, and Select all and Clear above them.
  • Mostly WarcraftIts own page: WoW Forever, my game UI, the backpack and the achievements.
  • SiteThe home page is just the tools, with a short Updates list and this changelog for the rest.
  • SiteText sizes and spacing evened out across the site, and tidier code underneath.
